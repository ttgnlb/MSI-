If you remember only one paragraph

Arnold argues that science, technology and medicine in colonial India were social and political, not neutral. They were not simply "diffused" from Britain to a passive India; they emerged from an interaction with Indian traditions, knowledge, labour and conditions. But this interaction took place within an unequal colonial relationship: the British used science for profit, control and legitimacy (the "civilising mission"), and denied it when it threatened their interests. Under the Company it was a "fitful flirtation"; in the steam age it became the Raj's proud monument; under the Crown it became "imperial science". Indians, meanwhile, made science their own — as a path to modernity and nationhood (Sircar, Ray, Bose, Raman), or rejected industrial modernity (Gandhi).
